## Dutchman Grave to Wet Bottom {#2J}

### 2017-11-05 route-finding {source="haz/triplog/2017-11-05.md"}
Tons of prints and wide trail to Sycamore Creek, and then nothing.
As expected, this (and almost every other) wash meant hunting for a trail on the other bank, because the Mazzies don't believe in straightforward crossings.
Trail got faint in Dry Wash, ended up just aiming for the bluffs and picking it back up there.
Then things just kinda rolled on until Wet Bottom Creek.

### 2017-11-05 water {source="haz/triplog/2017-11-05.md"}
Wet Bottom Creek was pretty yet quite dry.

## Wet Bottom to Red Creek {#2K}

### 2018-11-11 route-finding {source="haz/triplog/2018-11-11.md"}
Awkward shuffle to find the start of the trail along the bank, steep climb up loose rocks, and then the sign for the start of Wet Bottom.
It was a quick half mile.

### 2017-11-05 route-finding {source="haz/triplog/2017-11-05.md"}
From the Wet Bottom Trail junction it was time to head down to the river.
Trail gives up there so I rock-hopped to Red Creek Rapids.

## Red Creek to Highwater {#2N}

### 2018-11-11 route-finding {source="haz/triplog/2018-11-11.md"}
Tread was almost invisible, cairns were tiny and easy to miss, and everything was wildly overgrown.
Compared to Wet Bottom and Highwater this was by far the hardest to track.
Following it south offered good views of the river below and was mentally exhausting.
As soon as the route dropped below the upper banks of the river I was left trail-less so I ambled in the general direction of the rapids.
Thick reeds masked the way forward.

## Deadman Mesa to Highwater {#2P}

### 2018-12-06 tread {source="haz/triplog/2018-12-05.md"}
It was impeccable.
Seriously.
It is in at least, if not better, as good of condition as Highwater.
Shortly after the junction it cuts right down to the river with a series of rocky switchbacks and then, complete with good cairns, marches along the sandy banks with a few jumps to avoid different obstacles.
The first two miles has a lot of bovine traffic, and they stuck to the tread.
There were also long stretches of straight path through creosote.

### 2018-12-06 tread {source="haz/triplog/2018-12-05.md"}
Initial climb from the East Verde to Deadman Mesa Trail was steep and rocky and simple to follow.

## Twin Buttes Trailhead to Deadman Mesa {#2W}

### 2018-12-06 tread {source="haz/triplog/2018-12-05.md"}
Everyone complains about the rocks on this trail, yet the mud is so much worse.
Even a 20' section of mud would add pounds of clay to each boot, weighing me down and throwing my balance off.
Thankfully the trail is easy to follow.

### 2017-06-24 tread {source="haz/triplog/2017-06-24.md"}
As others have stated, this trail is rocky.
Rocks that rock, rocks that roll.
There are some genuinely good sections, where the rolly rocks ease up and you can gaze around without watching your step.

### 2017-06-24 route-finding {source="haz/triplog/2017-06-24.md"}
Only had to check GPS once or twice on some faint sections, otherwise this was a breeze to travel.

## Red Creek / Verde River Trail Junction {#node:1L}

### 2018-11-11 access {source="haz/triplog/2018-11-11.md"}
Crossed the Verde at Red Creek Rapids, water was about thigh deep.

### 2018-11-11 access {source="haz/triplog/2018-11-11.md"}
Confirmed the island was still there and that the upriver side would be easier to wade around than downriver.
Water was deeper and slower this time.
Got to the other side, walked a hundred feet just to bump into another channel and the realization that there were two parallel islands here and I had to do the whole ritual again.
All told it took me 90 minutes to finally get to the western bank.

### 2017-11-05 access {source="haz/triplog/2017-11-05.md"}
Hung my boots around my neck and crossed the Verde in thigh-deep current.
