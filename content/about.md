## Who I am

My name is Jacob, and I like to hike and backpack in wild, remote, and very quiet places.
This love for the outdoors started in the Upper Peninsula of Michigan, and then I moved to Arizona, and I adapted to the arid and challenging wilderness areas.
The Mazatzal Wilderness quickly attracted me, the massive range of mountains that were heavily damaged by multiple wildfires.
Plus, there was limited information about the wilderness online, so I just had to experience it for myself.

I came up with the idea for the "Mazatzal Wilderness Project" in 2017.
An ambitious, perhaps silly idea to walk every foot of every trail out there, regardless of the overgrowth, deadfall, and lack of maintenance.
I carried a GPS device on every hike, either a standalone device or an app on my phone, and these are the tracks on the site.

## The tracks

Those recordings are the source of every trail page and every route the builder can make.
The tracks are cleaned — obvious recording errors are dropped, and where I walked the same ground more than once the passes are averaged — but they are never redrawn.
If a trail on this site bends, it is because I bent with it.

Distances are measured along the recorded line.
Elevation comes from the USGS 3DEP lidar surface sampled along that line, which is steadier than what a handheld recorder reports.

## The condition notes

A condition note is something I saw on a particular day: brush closing in, deadfall across the tread, a spring running or dry, a stretch where the route is hard to follow.
Every note carries the date it was observed and was written by me, after walking it.
Notes accumulate rather than being overwritten, newest first, so you can see how a leg has changed.

## What this site does not do

- No accounts and nothing to sign up for.
- No saved routes. A route you build lives in its URL; copy the link and you have it.
- No tracking beyond cookieless page analytics.
