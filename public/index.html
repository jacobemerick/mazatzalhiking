How it works

Four decisions, one file.

No route-drawing. No guessing whether that dotted line on the map is still a trail. You pick the pieces; the builder handles the geometry.

Start somewhere

Pick a trailhead — Barnhardt, Deer Creek, Mount Peeley, City Creek, Sheep Bridge — or drop a point and find what's reachable from it.

Choose your trails

Select the segments you want to walk. Every one is drawn from a recorded track, not digitized off an old Forest Service quad.

Add what's worth seeing

Springs, saddles, camps, peaks, old cabins. Mark the ones you want the route to actually pass through.

Export and go

Get a single GPX or KML with the track, named waypoints, and condition notes baked into the descriptions.

Route shapes

Not every hike is a loop.

The Mazatzals don't always cooperate with a tidy circuit. Pick the shape that fits the trails, the water, and the time you have.

Loop

Out one trail, back another. Never repeat a mile.

Lasso

A shared stem into a loop and back out. Good when only one trail reaches the high country.

Figure-eight

Two loops off one junction. Bail after the first if the weather turns.

Out-and-back

Straight to the objective and home the same way. Often the only honest option.

Trail conditions

A line on the map is not a trail.

The Willow Fire in 2004 and the Bush Fire in 2020 rewrote big parts of this wilderness. Some trails came back. Some are catclaw and deadfall with a cairn every quarter mile if you're lucky.

Every segment carries notes about what it's actually like right now — brush, tread, route-finding, water. Build a route and you'll see the warnings before you're standing at the junction at 2pm wondering where the trail went.

Those notes travel with the file. Load the GPX and the waypoint descriptions come with you, no signal required.

Coverage

The trails, and the country around them.

Roughly 250,000 acres between the Verde River and the Beeline, from Mount Ord north to the Payson country.

Barnhardt #43 Mazatzal Divide #23 Y Bar Basin #44 Half Moon #55 Sandy Saddle #231 Rock Creek #42 Deer Creek #45 Cornucopia #86 Willow Spring #223 Verde River #11 Davenport #89 City Creek #414 North Peak #24 Brush #249 Red Hills #57 Sheep Creek #88 Club Cabin #26 The Park #96 + more as they get walked

Plus the places in between — Chilson Camp, Windsor Spring, Fisher Saddle, Horse Camp Seep, Mazatzal Peak, North Peak — and the Arizona Trail where it runs the divide.

This is wilderness. No cell service, seasonal water, and conditions that change with every fire and monsoon. A generated route is a plan, not a promise — carry a real map, a compass you can use, and more water than you think you need.
