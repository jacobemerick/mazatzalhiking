About this site

Where the lines come from

Every line on this site is a GPS track that one person walked and recorded. Nothing is traced from a Forest Service map, and nothing is copied from another site.

The tracks

I have hiked every trail in the Mazatzal Wilderness that appears here, carrying a GPS recorder. Those recordings are the source of every trail page and every route the builder can make. The tracks are cleaned — obvious recording errors are dropped, and where I walked the same ground more than once the passes are averaged — but they are never redrawn. If a trail on this site bends, it is because I bent with it.

Distances are measured along the recorded line. Elevation comes from the USGS 3DEP lidar surface sampled along that line, which is steadier than what a handheld recorder reports.

The condition notes

A condition note is something I saw on a particular day: brush closing in, deadfall across the tread, a spring running or dry, a stretch where the route is hard to follow. Every note carries the date it was observed and was written by me, after walking it. Notes accumulate rather than being overwritten, newest first, so you can see how a leg has changed.

Where a leg shows no note, nothing has been recorded about it. That is not the same as the trail being clear.

What this site does not do

  • No accounts and nothing to sign up for.
  • No saved routes. A route you build lives in its URL; copy the link and you have it.
  • No tracking beyond cookieless page analytics.

Get in touch

Corrections are welcome, especially about a trail whose condition has changed since I walked it. The site is developed in the open on GitHub, and the best place to tell me about a problem is an issue there.
