This is one of those double-glitter adventures.

Started off with a weird experience at Mormon Grove Trailhead. I followed a truck up to the trailhead, the whole time wondering who the heck was also heading up here at 5am, and then a third vehicle pulled up two minutes later. Both of those trucks had hunters in them scouting for mule deer. Three different groups heading out from Mormon Grove at 5am? That's probably more traffic than this spot gets all month.

## Saddle / Sheep Easy
First two miles by headlamp, rest was lit up by the waking eastern sun. Easy going, trail is in great shape. Minor recent erosion damage on the drop to McFarland Canyon.

## Sheep / Copper (Squaw Flat)
Maintenance on Sheep Creek trail was A+ awesome. Very easy to follow to the junction, only two annoying deadfall to work through. Copper Camp trail was slightly more difficult, yet easier than I remembered. Appreciated the unburnt sections of Copper Camp more today than I did on my first visit. Had some difficulty tracking the trail when it first climbed up to 5400', the cairns and tread didn't seem to be on the same route.

## Descent to Copper Camp
Tread was practically invisible, the big drop was a pure cairn-hunt. Once I reached the creek things got really annoying. I followed one or two river bank sections and fought through the thick growth, then boulder-hopped and fought with riparian growth. I was getting cranky during this section and probably would have benefitted from a breakfast / coffee break, instead just let the anxiety over the Cypress Ridge climb get to me. From my last hike along this trail I vaguely remembered that there were good sections of riverbank tread that are worth hunting out, didn't have the patience for that today. Reached the sheep corral and grumpily checked my water supply - I was down to two liters (started with five). Oof.

## Off-trail Climb to Cypress
Slow and steady. The climb up makes a lot of sense, follow the ridge as it zigs then zags. Found a shattered pair of sunglasses and old horseshoe, which was cool, and then, where the rocky section shows up, I found bone-a-fied tread! Switchbacks and all! Someone had spent some time building this up. Maybe it was bored sheep ranchers or something. I followed it when I could, lost it near the top and hacked through some thick brush to the saddle. The climb took longer than I thought, took lots of little breaks (played the game of "climb to that agave / rock pile / dead tree then breath and repeat"), and I made it to the top still feeling strong. Great views all around, etc.

## Off-trail Descent off Cypress
Them meadows are pretty cool, pushing through the brush to get to each one was annoying. Startled at least six mule deer, including one large-ish buck. Wondered how deep those hunters from this morning were willing to go, heh. Should have taken Ken's advice on the second meadow and swung further to the north, that waste-deep scrub hurt pushing through on my more-direct route. Found a small tank of water in a drainage and managed to pull two very-welcome liters from it. Final descent was more of a painful slow-motion tumble than a clean route. I could see cairns on the other side of Sheep Creek and bulled my way towards them.

## Sheep Creek
Swung over to the Round Spring area for a water refill, the creek was bone-dry but there was about 1" of water in the cement trough. It took a long time, even longer with the army of gnats swarming me, managed to pull four liters of yellow-tinted water out. A buff helped maintain my sanity, those little gnats were way to eager to get in my nose / ears / eyes / mouth. Freshly watered and fed I pushed out to Sheep Creek Trail, which was wonderfully clear and easy to walk with that maintenance. Man, that was a godsend, good tread and cairns all along that creek. Some thorny stuff is starting to encroach, and I probably kicked a half-dozen prickly pears along the way, and it was still a hundred times easier compared to my last visit. That last climb was tough, no switchbacks until the last few hundred feet, just steep, eroded tread to crawl up. Breaking out onto Squaw Flat felt like breaking the surface and then it was mostly downhill to McFarland, a quick break, and then the final four miles in darkness.

Water note: most of the "groundwater-fed" water sources were dry. A few sections of Copper Camp Creek & Sheep Creek had water, but they were few and far between, and some of them looked very nasty. Rock tank spots had some water from recent sprinkles, and the wildflowers were happy. Hope the weather this week recharges the western water tables.

Side note: after the crampfest in the Supes earlier this month I changed up my nutrition strategy to ALL THE SALT. Lots of tailwind combined with LMNT, think I only had 1L of "plain" water all day. Zero cramps, and I felt good all day, just your typical muscle tiredness / day-after soreness. Probably could have pushed more sugar / caffeine to speed up my pace, otherwise, very happy I was able to finish this loop in one day.
