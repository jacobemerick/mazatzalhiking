First time back to the grind after January's detour! After dropping kids off at school I made it to an empty trailhead at 9am and headed up Barnhardt. Things really dried up since I was last out here -- Garden Seep area was dry, both waterfalls silent, and only a few sad pools sat under Big Kahuna. The trail maintenance that @John10s and @kphoenix mentioned along Barnhardt was appreciated, the manzanita above the falls has been pushed back nicely. Reached Castersen Seep camp around 11:20a and watered up for an afternoon of work.

My main focus for this trip was going to be THE log... there's a large pine that fell 10+ years ago along the trail tread, and I was tired of balancing on it while getting shoved around with manzanita. I cut down manzanita on the downhill side, then chopped/saw/hacked/cried through the trunk until I was able to break the bottom third through and roll it off trail. Half of it was rot and was fairly easy to push aside, but the core was still solid and soaked in sap and took a fair bit of effort to get through. Along the way I found the largest black widow that ever widow'd, plus a few dozen wolf spiders and desert millipedes. Hopefully I didn't make any of them too angry.

I was just starting to work on the upper section of log, enjoying the cooling temperatures of late afternoon, when my darn lopper broke. I was working on the downhill side, cutting brush under the log, and a small shift in weight twisted the tool just enough to snap the blade. Second lopper that I've broken on this darn trail. That's my main tool out here, so yeah, guess my planned three-day outing was just "cut" short. After that setback I did some light cleaning and headed back to camp along the now-dark trail. Relaxing evening at camp with lots of filtering for tomorrow.

Slept solid in the hammock and woke up early, eager to enjoy the cool temps. With the loopers broke I only had a hatchet, saw, and digging hoe left. I was able to finish off that log (huzzah!) and clear "most" of that section of trail (still a cluster of skinny manzanita that needs to get cut back), and then I slowly walked back to camp, pulling up a few extra roots that had spring growth that I had previously skipped over, re-stacking some cairns, and improving the creek crossings. Once I got back to camp I still had some oomph left in me, so I went ahead and tracked down exactly where the trail crosses Barnhardt Creek for future trips. Had to bushwhack uphill to find the tread, but once found, I was able to crawl along it (lots of deadfall) and find where it met the creek. That's going to be a beautiful section of trail once cleared.

Back at camp I did some quick filtering and then headed out. Quiet hike down, no one on trail, and empty trailhead. It may have been cut short but hey, it was still a lot of fun out there, and there was no knee issues this time.
